## Risk Summary

Ford carries the risk profile of a leveraged industrial with a captive finance arm attached,
and the two components fail in different ways. Headline net debt/EBITDA of 8.9x overstates
industrial leverage because it consolidates Ford Credit, whose debt is match-funded against a
receivables book. Interest coverage of 2.1x does not have that excuse: it is a thin cushion
for a business running a 3.1% operating margin into a cyclical end market.

## Downside Scenarios

**Base case ($10.85 entry).** Realised 30-day volatility of 34.8% with a beta of 1.61 implies
a one-standard-deviation annual range of roughly $7.07 to $14.63. The 52-week range of $9.06
to $14.85 is consistent with that distribution, so the market is not currently pricing a
regime shift.

**Adverse case (roughly -25%, $8.15).** A cyclical downturn compresses the 3.1% operating
margin toward breakeven while the $8.8B of incremental UAW labour cost through 2028 is fixed.
Ford Credit delinquencies at 1.42% versus 1.08% a year ago is a 31% relative deterioration and
a leading indicator of consumer credit stress; provisioning against a larger book would
compound the industrial decline rather than offset it.

**Severe case (roughly -45%, $6.00).** A recession that pushes delinquencies past 2% while
industrial margin goes negative forces a dividend cut and a capital raise. The 5-year max
drawdown of 58.2% establishes that the equity has traded through this scenario before, so it
is a historical outcome rather than a modelled extreme.

## Risk Decomposition

**Financial risk: HIGH.** Debt-to-equity of 3.62 and interest coverage of 2.1x leave little
room for EBITDA compression. Total debt of $158.2B against $23.0B of cash means refinancing is
continuous rather than episodic, so the balance sheet is directly exposed to the rate path.

**Operating risk: HIGH.** A 3.1% operating margin means a 310bps adverse move in the cost base
eliminates operating profit entirely. Warranty and recall costs of $4.8B against a $2.3B
historical run-rate is a 109% overrun that has persisted long enough to be treated as
structural rather than as a one-time item. EV losses of $5.1B annually with production targets
cut twice is capital being consumed by a segment that is shrinking against plan.

**Liquidity risk: MODERATE.** Free cash flow of $6.70B against $5.88B of net income is
adequate conversion, and $23.0B of cash covers near-term obligations. This is the one
category that does not currently constrain the equity.

**Volatility risk: HIGH.** Beta of 1.61 with 34.8% realised volatility places this in the top
decile of large-cap volatility. A 14-day ATR of $0.38 on a $10.85 share price is 3.5% daily
range, so any stop inside 7% will be triggered by noise.

## Position Sizing

A 2x ATR stop sits at $10.09, 7.0% below entry. Given 34.8% realised volatility, volatility-
scaled sizing argues for roughly half of a normal position; the combination of high beta, thin
interest coverage and a deteriorating credit indicator does not support full weight. The
binding review trigger is the Ford Credit delinquency rate — a print above 1.75% changes the
severe-case probability materially and should force a re-evaluation of the position rather
than a re-evaluation of the stop.
