Texas Instruments is in a multi-year earnings contraction driven by volume decline and
fixed-cost absorption, with cash conversion holding up better than the income statement
suggests. Revenue of $15.64B is down 10.7% year over year and down 21.9% from the 2022 peak
of $20.03B, a third consecutive year of decline. Net income of $4.80B is down 26.2%, falling
roughly 2.5x faster than revenue, which is the signature of an under-absorbed fixed cost base
rather than of pricing pressure alone.

Margin compression confirms that reading. Gross margin fell 500bps to 57.9% and operating
margin fell 670bps to 35.2%. Operating margin declining faster than gross margin means
operating expense is not scaling down with revenue. Against $4.82B of capital expenditure on
$15.64B of revenue — 30.8% capital intensity — this is a company absorbing a large capacity
build into a declining demand environment, which is the worst possible sequencing for
reported earnings.

Cash conversion is the one genuinely positive item. Operating cash flow of $6.32B against
$4.80B of net income is an OCF/NI ratio of 1.32, and the quarterly operating cash flow path
of $1.50B, $1.48B, $1.71B and $1.63B is stable while quarterly net income fell from $1.36B to
$1.21B in the back half. Earnings are cash-backed. Free cash flow of $1.50B is weak, but the
weakness is entirely capex, not conversion.

The quarterly sequence shows stabilisation rather than continued deterioration. Revenue rose
from $3.59B in Q1 to $4.15B in Q3 before easing to $4.08B in Q4, and the trailing two-quarter
average is above the first-half average. Net income of $1.21B in Q4 against $1.10B in Q1 is
modestly higher. The rate of decline is slowing even though the year-over-year comparison
remains firmly negative.
