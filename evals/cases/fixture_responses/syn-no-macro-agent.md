### Bull Case

The earnings profile is unusually clean for a retailer. Revenue up 5.8% with US comps at 4.6%
and eCommerce at 22%, operating margin up 35bps, and OCF/NI at 1.06 then 1.09. Consensus has
been revised up 3.2% over three months. Growth accompanied by margin expansion and cash
conversion above 1.0x is the combination the earnings factor is built to detect.

Share gains are coming from higher-income households, which is the durable form of the
trade-down story rather than the cyclical one. Those customers carry higher basket values and
better attach rates into eCommerce and advertising, so the mix shift compounds. The DCF at
$114.00 is driven by exactly this: margin expansion from the advertising and marketplace mix
rather than from unit growth.

The moat is structural. Procurement scale plus a store network that doubles as fulfilment
infrastructure cannot be replicated at the required density, and the beta of 0.62 with net
debt/EBITDA at 1.6x on laddered maturities means the equity carries little financing risk.

### Bear Case

Gross margin is levered to grocery pricing in a way that is easy to underestimate. The risk
report puts 100bps of price investment at roughly 4% of EPS, so a deflationary grocery
environment that forces price investment does real damage to the earnings line that the
entire thesis depends on. The 5th-percentile scenario at $74.00 is 16% below entry.

The technical setup is the weakest leg. Price is 11.3% above the 200-day SMA with RSI at 64.8
and a Bollinger position of 0.78, which is an extended entry rather than a fresh one. Buying
extended into a 1.0x position means the $84.95 stop is only 3.6% away and a routine mean
reversion takes the position out.

The absence of a macro read is itself a limitation. Retail demand is rate- and
employment-sensitive, and this decision was made without a regime input that would normally
carry 0.12 of the weight and act as a conviction multiplier.

Q4 earnings is the confirming event; the thesis requires US comps above 3.5% and continued
